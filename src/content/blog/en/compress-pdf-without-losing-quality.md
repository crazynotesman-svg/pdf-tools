Oversized PDFs are a daily annoyance: an email bounces because the attachment is too large, a portal rejects your upload, and downloads on mobile crawl. Before you resort to screenshotting pages, it helps to understand why PDFs grow so big – and how to reduce PDF file size properly without ruining the quality.

## Why are PDF files so large?

A PDF is rarely just text. The usual size culprits are:

- **Embedded images:** Photos and high-resolution graphics often make up 80–90 percent of a file.
- **Fonts:** Some documents embed entire font families so they render identically everywhere.
- **Redundant objects:** Files saved from office suites often carry duplicate data, empty regions, or unneeded metadata.
- **Scans:** A scanned page is one big image – and stored uncompressed, it becomes huge fast.

The more of these a document contains, the bigger the file. The good news: a lot of it can be removed without anyone noticing the difference.

## How PDF compression works in your browser

Compression doesn't have to happen on a server. Modern PDF tools that run entirely in the browser process the file directly on your device. Two approaches are common:

**Standard compression:** The PDF is rebuilt – redundant objects and metadata are stripped, and the structure is optimized. Text stays searchable and selectable, and quality is fully preserved. This is ideal for documents with redundant data.

**Strong compression:** Every page is re-created as an image and stored with stronger compression. This yields the biggest reduction – but the text becomes a picture, so it can no longer be selected or searched.

## When should you use which method?

- **Standard compression** fits contracts, invoices, application documents – anything you may need to edit or search later.
- **Strong compression** pays off when size is everything: email attachments, upload forms, or archives you only need to read.

A note on honesty: pure text PDFs are already very compact. If such a file barely shrinks, that's not the tool's fault – it's the nature of the document.

## Privacy: why local processing matters

Documents often contain sensitive data – contract terms, addresses, billing details. When a PDF is uploaded to a server, it leaves your computer and you have to trust the provider. With in-browser processing, that never happens: the file stays on your device, and nothing remains after you close the page. That's a real advantage for privacy-conscious users, especially with company documents.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I lose quality when compressing a PDF?

Not with standard compression: text stays sharp and searchable, and images keep their resolution. The strong variant can slightly reduce quality – choose it only when file size matters more.

### What's the difference between standard and strong compression?

Standard rebuilds the PDF losslessly and preserves text. Strong converts every page into an image for the biggest reduction – at the cost of text selection.

### Why is my PDF barely getting smaller?

It probably contains mostly text with few images. Such files are already compact, so there is little to gain.
